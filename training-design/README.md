# Emergency and disaster training design

**Maintainer:** John Quinn, MD, MPH, PhD, EMT-P  
**Version:** 0.1.0 · **Updated:** 22 October 2026  
**Status:** original portfolio demonstration; not a validated instrument or approved operational policy. Synthetic examples only.

A worked example of designing and evaluating a short **simulated coordination and handover exercise**. It demonstrates educational design, not treatment protocols, certification or an authorised clinical scope.

## Curriculum map

| Objective | Learning activity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identify missing handover information | Facilitated fictional briefing | Learner identifies gaps and records questions |
| Communicate responsibilities clearly | Role-play between sender and receiver | Observer notes named roles and closed-loop confirmation |
| Manage an unresolved coordination problem | Scenario inject | Team describes escalation and contingency |
| Turn reflection into an improvement action | Structured debrief | Action has an owner, review date and measure |

## Suggested 60-minute session

- 0–10 min: briefing, psychological safety, objectives and roles.
- 10–20 min: worked example and questions.
- 20–35 min: [scenario](scenario.md).
- 35–50 min: debrief using observed behaviours.
- 50–60 min: repeat one difficult segment, feedback and next actions.

Roles: facilitator, sending coordinator, receiving coordinator, observer. Adapt timing and group size to learner needs. Provide a glossary and translated materials with review by a competent bilingual reviewer when needed.

Use the [assessment and debrief worksheet](assessment.md). Pilot the session and revise before broader use. No patient, employer or operational records are needed.
