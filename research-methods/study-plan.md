## Protocol worksheet

| Field | Complete before recruitment |
|---|---|
| Decision | What decision will the results inform? |
| Research question | Population, setting, topic and time period |
| Design | Survey, interview, focus group, Delphi, or specified combination |
| Primary outcome | Exact construct, measurement, unit and timing |
| Population | Eligibility and exclusions, with justification |
| Sampling frame | Source of eligible participants and coverage gaps |
| Sampling strategy | Probability or non-probability method; why it fits |
| Sample size | Precision, power or qualitative adequacy rationale |
| Recruitment | Who invites, power relationships and voluntary participation |
| Analysis | Predefined comparisons, missing-data rules and limitations |
| Governance | Review determination, consent, access and retention |
| Dissemination | Audience, participant feedback and permitted outputs |

## Worked teaching example

Question: Which learning activities do adult course participants find useful, and why?

Design: short post-course survey plus optional interviews. A convenience sample of attendees can describe respondents' experience, but cannot establish population prevalence or a causal training effect. Collecting only favourable volunteers would introduce selection bias.

Before a real study, define eligible attendees and invite consistently. Record counts invited, eligible, responding and analysed, using the correct denominator for each rate. Compare available respondent characteristics with the sampling frame where appropriate and permitted. Describe non-response and coverage limitations.

For interviews, purposefully seek variation in experience and perspectives. Specify how the team will assess whether the material is sufficient for the question, rather than prescribing an arbitrary universal sample size.

## Analysis plan

- Summarise categorical responses with counts and denominators.
- Show ordinal rating distributions; a median alone can hide disagreement.
- Distinguish skipped, not-applicable and invalid values.
- Predefine subgroup comparisons and avoid revealing small identifiable groups.
- Use inferential analyses only when design and assumptions support them; document effect estimates and uncertainty, not only p-values.
- Keep exploratory findings separate from prespecified analyses.
- Do not treat satisfaction or self-rated confidence as evidence of clinical competence or patient benefit.
