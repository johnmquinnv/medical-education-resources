# Survey, interview and focus-group templates

**Maintainer:** John Quinn, MD, MPH, PhD, EMT-P  
**Version:** 0.1.0 · **Updated:** 22 October 2026  
**Status:** original portfolio demonstration; not a validated instrument or approved operational policy. Synthetic examples only.

## Participant introduction — adapt before real use

Explain who is conducting the activity, its purpose, expected duration, voluntary nature, any risks, who can access responses, retention, withdrawal limits and contact route. Explain whether responses are anonymous or confidential accurately. Do not claim anonymity if login, email collection or combinations of responses can identify people.

## Short survey: adult learning activities

1. Which activity are you rating? Simulation / facilitated case discussion / independent reading.
2. How useful was it for the stated learning objective? 1 Not at all useful / 2 Slightly / 3 Moderately / 4 Very / 5 Extremely useful / Not applicable.
3. How confident are you that you can explain the process taught? 1 Not at all confident to 5 Extremely confident / Not applicable.
4. What helped your learning? Optional free text.
5. What should change next time? Optional free text.

Pilot wording, accessibility and completion time. Avoid combining two questions into one. Do not request clinical cases or identifiers in free text. These items are illustrative and unvalidated.

## Structured interview guide

- Describe the activity in your own words.
- Which part supported your learning? Can you give a non-identifying example?
- Which part was difficult or unclear?
- How did time, language, equipment or group size affect participation?
- What would you retain and what would you change?
- What have we missed?

Use neutral probes: “Can you explain further?” and “Was that typical or unusual?” Record interviewer role, relationship to participants and deviations from the guide.

## Focus-group facilitation plan

Use the same core topics with a moderator and note-taker. Set expectations for respectful discussion and explain that the research team cannot guarantee other participants will maintain confidentiality. Invite differing views, manage dominant voices and avoid forcing consensus. Record consent for any recording separately. Keep a reflexive memo about group dynamics and the moderator's influence.
