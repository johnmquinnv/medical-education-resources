# Question-bank study strategy

A provider-neutral framework that works with any question bank.

## Principles

Two techniques are repeatedly supported in the medical-education literature:

- **Active recall:** test yourself rather than re-reading or highlighting.
- **Spaced repetition:** revisit material at increasing intervals. One 2024 study of clinical problem solving found spaced repetition improved learning and scores ([Bahria University study, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186069/)).

## Workflow

1. **Baseline:** do a short mixed block early to find weak areas.
2. **Daily blocks:** timed, mixed-topic blocks under exam-like conditions.
3. **Review every question,** including those you got right. Ask why the right answer is right and why each distractor is wrong.
4. **Log errors** in a simple table (see below), grouped by cause.
5. **Convert errors to flashcards** and review them on a spaced schedule.
6. **Revisit weak topics** weekly, and re-test them in a new block.
7. **Full-length practice** exams close to the real test to build stamina and pacing.

## Error log template

| Date | Topic | Question stem (short) | Why I got it wrong | Correct reasoning | Flashcard made |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | Knowledge gap / misread / rushed / anchoring | | Y/N |

Common causes: knowledge gap, misreading the stem, anchoring, time pressure, and guessing between two options.

## Weekly plan example

- **Mon-Fri:** one or two timed blocks plus review, and flashcards daily.
- **Saturday:** weak-topic block and error-log review.
- **Sunday:** rest or light flashcards only.

## Tips for shift workers

- Use short blocks (10-20 questions) around shifts rather than skipping days.
- Keep flashcards on your phone for short gaps.
- Protect sleep before and after nights; avoid heavy study immediately after a night shift.

## Avoid

- Passive re-reading as the main method.
- Memorising answers to repeated questions without understanding the reasoning.
- Running out of time for review: reviewing is where most learning happens.
