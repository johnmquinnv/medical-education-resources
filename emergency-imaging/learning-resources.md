# Emergency imaging: learning resources

**Version:** 1.1.0 · **Last editorial update:** 22 October 2026  
**Maintainer:** John Quinn, MD, MPH, PhD, EMT-P. Educational draft; see the [repository review status](../README.md).

A curated starting list. Availability, cost and licensing change, so check each site.

## General radiology and X-ray / CT

- [Radiopaedia](https://radiopaedia.org/): peer-reviewed, collaborative radiology reference and cases. It also offers an [Emergency Radiology course](https://radiopaedia.org/courses/emergency-radiology-course-online/).
- [LITFL Radiology Library](https://litfl.com/Radiology/): plain X-ray and CT requests, interpretation and clinical cases.
- [European Society of Radiology: Modern Radiology](https://www.myesr.org/app/uploads/2025/05/ESR_Modern_eBook_25.pdf): free educational eBook from the ESR.

## Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS)

- [ACEP Emergency Ultrasound Section resources](https://www.acep.org/emultrasound/resources/) and [ACEP Sonoguide](https://www.acep.org/by-medical-focus/ultrasound).
- [POCUS Med Ed](https://www.pocusmeded.com/): free tutorials for common emergency presentations.
- [Indiana University POCUS continuing education](https://medicine.iu.edu/emergency-medicine/continuing-education/pocus): includes free ultrasound flashcards.
- [Society of Point of Care Ultrasound education resources](https://www.pocus.org/educational-resources/): directory of training providers and certification pathways.

## A systematic approach (example: chest X-ray)

Use a consistent order every time, for example:

1. Patient details, projection and image quality
2. Airway and mediastinum
3. Breathing: lungs and pleura
4. Circulation: heart size and contour
5. Diaphragm and below
6. Everything else: bones, soft tissues, lines and tubes
7. Review areas: apices, behind the heart, costophrenic angles, under the diaphragm

Adapt the same principle to trauma series, abdominal films, and CT head and cervical spine.

## Suggested learning plan

- **Weekly:** review a handful of cases from a case library and write down your own read before looking at the answer.
- **Monthly:** a focused topic (for example, cervical spine, pneumothorax, free fluid on FAST).
- **Hands-on:** supervised ultrasound practice and local credentialing, because reading about POCUS is not a substitute for scanning under supervision.

## Notes

- Check **both patient confidentiality/consent and permission to reuse the image**. A copyright licence does not replace confidentiality safeguards, and anonymisation does not grant copyright permission.
- For this public repository, use synthetic examples or links to the original provider. Do not upload patient images.
- For teaching elsewhere, assess whether the patient could be identified from the image, metadata, accompanying text or context; obtain consent where required for the intended audience and use, and comply with the image owner's licence or permission. Follow local governance requirements and [GMC guidance on recordings in public media](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/making-and-using-visual-and-audio-recordings-of-patients/recordings-for-use-in-widely-accessible-public-media---television-radio-internet-print).
- Imaging interpretation in practice should follow local protocols and formal radiology reporting.
